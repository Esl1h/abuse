# Object types that light the room around them. Phase 6, block 6.4.
# See src/render/dynlight.h.
#
# One entry per line:
#
#   NAME   radius   strength
#
# NAME is the object type as data/lisp spells it in def_char, radius is the
# reach in world pixels, and strength is how many light levels are added at
# the centre, out of 63. The light falls off to nothing at the radius and
# never takes a pixel past full brightness, so a lit room does not change.
#
# The numbers are deliberately small. This is a flash in a dark corridor,
# not a lamp: a shot that lights the whole screen reads as a bug, and the
# levels were drawn for a game where almost nothing moved the light.

# A name that the loaded data does not define is ignored, so this table can
# name everything any data set has. `--dump-dynlight` prints which ones
# resolved.

# Shots. The flash at the muzzle is the first tick of the shot's life, and
# then the light travels with it, which is what a tracer does anyway.
#
# SHOTGUN_BULLET is the first one that matters: fire_object makes one of
# those for weapon 0, the laser everybody starts with and the only weapon
# with unlimited ammunition.
MBULLET             26   16
SHOTGUN_BULLET      30   20
PLASMAGUN_BULLET    40   26
LSABER_BULLET       36   24
DFRIS_BULLET        34   22

# In flight. The rocket lighting the corridor ahead of it is most of what
# makes it feel heavy.
ROCKET              48   28
STRAIT_ROCKET       48   28
GRENADE             30   14
FIREBOMB            40   24

# Explosions already drop an EXP_LIGHT of their own, which is a real light
# source and brighter than this. These are the frames around it, so that the
# blast does not go dark a tick before the sprite does.
EXPLODE1            56   20
EXPLODE2            48   18
EXPLODE3            40   16
EXPLODE5            56   20
